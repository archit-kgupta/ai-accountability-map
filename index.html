How do organisations construct accountability for AI-assisted decisions in UK finance?

Research question: How do organisations giving evidence to the UK Treasury Committee's 2025–26 inquiry into AI in financial services construct accountability for regulated firms' AI-assisted decisions?

An evidence map of written and oral evidence to the House of Commons Treasury Committee's inquiry into AI in financial services (launched February 2025; report HC 684 published January 2026). Coded by Archit Gupta (University of Bristol) as an independent project alongside his undergraduate dissertation. 82 of the 82 published written submissions coded; the 4 oral evidence sessions are not yet coded. Codes follow codebook.md in this project's GitHub repository.

Definitions and method

  • Accountability follows Bovens (2007, European Law Journal 13(4)): a relationship in which an actor is answerable to a forum, must explain and justify its conduct, and can face consequences. The map records to whom each organisation assigns accountability for a regulated firm's AI-assisted decision, and whether it judges existing rules sufficient.
  • Constructing accountability means the positions organisations put forward about who is, or should be, answerable. They are positions in a policy debate, not evidence of practice inside firms.
  • Coding uses a framework (deductive) coding scheme: every contribution is coded against fixed, predefined categories in the codebook, rather than codes emerging from the text.
  • Actor groups. Each submitter keeps its original actor type and is also placed in one of three groups: industry (financial-services firms and trade bodies), public authorities (FCA, Bank of England, HM Treasury) and public-interest and independent contributors (consumer, civil-society, academic and think tank). Tech and AI providers and consultancies are outside the SM&CR perimeter, so they form a separate category: they stay in the table but are left out of the group charts (21 submissions). This is a stated limitation.
